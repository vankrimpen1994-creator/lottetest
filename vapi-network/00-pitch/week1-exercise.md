# The Week 1 exercise — what Rik asked for, and how to hand it over

## The ask, in his words
> "One week, one week of content, so we at least see: okay, she understands vAPI, she
> understands what kind of calendar we want."

He's not buying a strategy document. He's testing three things:
1. **Can she read a blurb and understand the product without hand-holding?** He said it
   twice — the thing they keep hitting with social media people is needing to babysit.
2. **Does the calendar look like something that could convert?** His words: "that looks
   good, that could convert, but in any case that looks good."
3. **Can she do the graphics herself?** Not essential to him, but it's the friction point
   he named: "I need a graphic designer, I can't put this together myself."

So the deliverable answers all three: nine finished posts, a carousel specced
slide-by-slide, and visual briefs Lotte can execute in Canva without a designer.

## What's in the file
Seven tabs. `Start here` is written for Rik, not for us — he should be able to open it
cold and understand the whole approach in ninety seconds.

| Tab | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Start here | The strategy on one screen: account split, three pillars, the B2B/B2C judgement call, counts |
| Week 1 calendar | The grid. Nine rows, colour-coded B2B/B2C, with hook, pain point, CTA and a "why this post" per row |
| Post copy | All nine posts in full, ready to paste |
| Carousel — Monday | Slide-by-slide copy and design notes for the week's carousel |
| Daily engagement | The commenting routine and who to target |
| Measurement | What we track from day one, with honest benchmarks |
| Questions for Rik | Nine things to confirm, ordered by what blocks most |

## The structure, and why
**9 posts across 3 accounts, Mon–Fri.**

| Account | Posts | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Mark (Founder 1) | 3 | B2B only |
| [Founder 2] | 4 | 2 × B2B, 2 × B2C |
| Company page | 2 | Informational / credibility |

That matches what Lotte and Rik agreed on the call: founders are the main channel, the
company page exists so the brand looks real, and B2C runs at a deliberately smaller volume.

**The one judgement call to be ready to defend.** On the call Lotte said she'd prefer to
keep B2B and B2C on separate profiles, and Rik liked that. The brief then asked for one
founder to carry both. Those aren't actually in conflict, and the answer is worth saying
out loud because it's the kind of reasoning he's testing for:

> Founder 2 carries both audiences, but the B2C posts stay inside the same three pillars —
> the freelancer's side of getting paid for scoped work. The subject never changes, only
> who's being addressed. Topic authority holds, and we don't spin up a fourth account
> nobody has time to run. If the freelancer side ever needs real volume, that's the
> moment to split it out.

## Things deliberately left blank
Everything in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`. Two kinds, and both are arguments for her rather than
against:
- `[CONFIRM WITH RIK: …]` — a fact we won't publish on a guess. There are five.
- `[MARK: …]` / `[FOUNDER 2: …]` — a line only the founder can write: a real number, a
  real opinion. LinkedIn's ranking model reportedly penalises text that reads as purely
  machine-written, so those gaps are load-bearing.

If he reads the blanks as gaps in the work, the answer is: every one of them is a place
where inventing something would have been easy and wrong.

>   The main one: is the hiring/escrow product called Tasks, and is it live? I've

>
> Happy to walk through it, or just take the comments in the sheet.

## If he asks "how long did this take?"
Answer honestly and don't undersell it. The research and the system behind it are
reusable; week 2 takes a fraction of the time because the pillars, voice and templates
already exist. That's the actual pitch for a recurring engagement.
