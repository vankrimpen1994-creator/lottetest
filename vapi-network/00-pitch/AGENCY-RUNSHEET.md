# Agency Meeting Run-Sheet — 30 Minutes

**Who's in the room:** the agency that manages vAPI Network. **Not the client.**
**What they're deciding:** whether to hand us content work for their clients.
**What they're buying:** a production system they can point at any client and not
have to supervise.

---

## The frame that wins this meeting

They are not testing whether we know vAPI Network. They're testing whether we can
**take a client's goals and turn them into a content plan that gets executed reliably.**

So: **lead with the system. Use vAPI as the worked example.**

The strongest single move available is to show them a complete, sourced, 90-day plan for
one of their clients, built in a day, and then say: *this is what our intake and strategy
process produces, for any client, in the first week.* The plan is the artefact. **The
system is the pitch.**

Deep client knowledge, deployed too early, actually works against us here — it makes us
look like a one-client specialist rather than a supplier they can scale with.

---

## 0:00–0:03 — Frame

> "Before we get into it — we understand we're talking to you, not to vAPI. So rather
> than pitch you on their product, we've done something more useful. We ran one of your
> clients through our actual intake and strategy process, start to finish, and brought
> the output. Then you can judge the process on something real instead of on a deck.
> Stop me whenever."

Don't open with credentials. Open with the thing on the table.

---

## 0:03–0:12 — The system _(share `HOW-WE-WORK.md`)_

Walk the five stages. Keep it to the shape — don't read the document.

**1 · Intake.** Structured brief. Written client profile, **every claim sourced.**
> "Here's what that catches. On vAPI, intake surfaced a name collision — there's a
> much larger company called vapi.ai, YC-backed, raised $50M in May, a million
> developers. LinkedIn search is name-match weighted, so their page is effectively
> invisible behind it. Free to fix, and it hadn't been flagged. That's the kind of thing
> a real intake catches in week one."

_This is now evidence of process, not the headline. That's the right altitude for this room._

**2 · Strategy.** Positioning, message hierarchy, five pillars with a **fixed weekly
ratio**, voice guide with a banned-words list, KPIs with stated assumptions.
> "The pillar ratio is the quality control that matters most. It's the thing that stops
> month two turning into four product announcements a week — which is how most client
> content dies."

**3 · Production.** The weekly rhythm — one job per session, each with a written
procedure. **Drafted a week ahead, always.**

**4 · QC.** Show them the checklist.
> "The one I'd point at: no invented metrics, quotes or case studies, ever. If we don't
> have a number, it's marked as missing, not estimated. Your client's credibility is
> what's on the line, and it's your name in front of them, not ours."

**5 · Reporting.** Weekly one-screen, monthly dashboard — **written so you can forward
them to the client unedited.**

Then the transferability point, explicitly:
> "None of that is vAPI-specific. Swap the profile and the pillars and it runs for any
> client you've got. You're not hiring someone who knows one product."

---

## 0:12–0:22 — The worked example _(share `90-DAY-PLAN.md`)_

Frame it as output, not as a pitch to the client.

> "So here's what came out for vAPI. Their proposition is a task market — you hire
> agents or humans for scoped work at a fixed price, and the money sits in USDC escrow
> until the work's accepted. Disputes go to three reviewers.
>
> The strategic call we made: **lead with the hiring problem, never with the protocol.**
> Everyone in that category writes about the payment rails. But 'you've been burned by a
> contractor, here's what escrow changes' is a story that works on anyone who's ever
> hired someone. That's the difference between content their market reads and content
> their engineers read."

Then the shape, fast:
- Two-sided market → content recruits buyers *and* suppliers on a fixed ratio
- **Week 0** foundation → **M1** foundation and signal → **M2** traction → **M3** conversion
- **The podcast is the engine, not a side project.** One 45-minute recording → 8–12 LinkedIn assets, and the invite list *is* the target list
- Design is templated: six masters in week 0, then 12–16 assets a month from them

**Then the numbers — as bands, with the condition attached:**
> "From a standing start: month one 300–600 followers and 8–15k impressions; month two
> 800–1,500 and 30–60k with 3–8 qualified conversations; month three 1,500–3,000 and
> 60–120k, 10–25 qualified conversations.
>
> Those are bands, not promises, and the assumptions are written down next to them. The
> big one: it needs founders posting from personal profiles. LinkedIn suppresses company
> page reach badly. If it's a page posting alone from zero, those numbers don't happen,
> and I'd rather you hear that from us now than from the client in month two."

_Optional, if they're engaged:_ show `SAMPLE-POSTS.md` — three finished posts. Proves the
voice in sixty seconds, which no amount of strategy talk does.

---

## 0:22–0:27 — Working together

> "Practically: **you own the client relationship. We're invisible.** White-label by
> default, our name appears nowhere unless you want it to. One contact on our side,
> no account-manager chain. Forty-eight hour turnaround on in-scope revisions, two
> rounds per asset.
>
> And we need one named approver with a 48-hour window. Unreturned means approved —
> agreed up front, so the calendar can't be blocked by silence. That's usually the
> thing that kills content programmes, and it's worth settling in the contract rather
> than discovering it in week five."

**Be direct about AI, because they'll wonder and it explains the price:**
> "We use AI heavily — research, first drafts, turning a podcast transcript into a dozen
> assets. That's how the volume works at this price. Strategy, judgement, relationships
> and a review of every word are human. A person is accountable for everything that
> ships. Happy to walk through exactly where the line is."

**Then the confidence move:**
> "One more thing, and it's against our own interest to say it. If at week six
> engagement is still under 2% and there are no qualified conversations, we stop and
> re-plan rather than keep billing for posts. We'd rather be measured than retained."

---

## 0:27–0:30 — Close

> "Two questions. First — is the $1,300 per project monthly, or for the whole engagement?
> It's a three-times difference in what we'd commit to and I'd rather scope it honestly
> than over-promise.
>
> Second — if this works for vAPI, is VAPA the same shape, and how many other clients
> could this run for?"

That second question is the one that matters. **It moves the conversation from a trial
to a pipeline**, and it tells us what they're actually shopping for.

Then **book the next thing before leaving the call.** A trial with no next calendar
entry dies.

## If you get 10 minutes instead of 30
The five stages in two minutes → the vAPI plan as the worked example → the numbers with
the founder condition → white-label and turnaround terms → the two closing questions.
Send the documents afterwards.

## Tabs to have open
1. `HOW-WE-WORK.md` ← **share this first**
2. `90-DAY-PLAN.md` ← the worked example

## Do not
- Don't open by demonstrating vAPI knowledge. It reads as a one-client specialist.
- Don't pitch the client's strategy *to* the agency as if they were the client.
- Don't promise numbers without the founder-participation condition attached.
- Don't leave without asking how many clients this could run for.
