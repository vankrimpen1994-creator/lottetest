## The thesis in four lines

1. **Lead with the hiring problem, never the protocol.** vAPI is a place to hire agents or humans for scoped work at a fixed price, with money in USDC escrow until the work is accepted. That's a story anyone who's hired a contractor understands. "Non-custodial x402 client" is not. Everyone else in this category writes the second sentence.
2. It's a **two-sided market**, so content recruits buyers *and* suppliers, on a fixed ratio.
3. **LinkedIn is empty.** Every competitor is on Crypto Twitter — and a task marketplace is fundamentally a future-of-work story, which is a LinkedIn story.
4. The **podcast is the engine, not a side project.** One 45-minute recording → 8–12 LinkedIn assets, and the invite list *is* the target list.

---

## The spine of the content: the Tasks flow

Everything in pillar one explains one of these five steps:

1. Work is scoped. **Both sides sign the scope and the price.**
2. The buyer **locks the agreed USDC in escrow.**
3. Work is delivered and reviewed.
4. **Accepted work releases payment** and records the outcome.
5. **Disputes go to three reviewers. Two matching votes release, refund, or split.**

Fixed price, money held safely, a review step, a defined way to settle a disagreement.
It maps directly onto problems every company already has with contractors — which is why
it works on an audience with zero crypto knowledge.

## Phase 0 — Week 0: Foundation (before a single post)

Unglamorous and non-negotiable. Skipping it is why most LinkedIn launches stall at week five.

**Fix the name collision first.** `vapi.ai` — voice AI, YC W21, $50M Series B in May 2026, 1M+ developers — owns every "VAPI" search on LinkedIn, where search is name-match weighted.
- Page name **"vAPI Network"**, never "VAPI" alone; tagline carries "onchain task market"
- Claim the custom page URL immediately
- "vAPI Network" in every post's first line and every image watermark
- Own `#x402` / `#agenticpayments` / `#agenteconomy`, never `#vapi`

**Also in Week 0:**
- Company page built: banner, logo, tagline, About, Products, custom CTA
- **Founder/exec profile overhauls** — headline, banner, About, Featured. This is where reach comes from; the page is the credibility backstop
- Brand kit: 6 templates (quote, stat, carousel cover, carousel interior, clip frame, podcast announce)
- Analytics baseline, tracking sheet, UTM convention
- 30-day calendar approved **in one batch**, so weeks 1–4 never wait on sign-off
- Podcast: name, cover art, format, first 10 guest targets, recording stack

## Month 1 — Foundation & Signal (Weeks 1–4)

**Goal:** publish consistently, establish the voice, learn what this audience responds to. We're buying information this month, not leads.

**Cadence:** 4 company + 3 founder posts per week (28 total)

| Week | Focus | Notable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How work gets commissioned** — the escrow model, plainly | Launch post; carousel: *"What actually happens when you hire through escrow"* |
| 2 | **Trust & Control** — counterparty risk on both sides, the dispute process | Poll: *"What's gone wrong most often when you've hired a contractor?"* → doubles as ICP research |
| 3 | **Supply-side** — "the money was locked before you started" | **Podcast pilot records.** First direct recruitment push to freelancers and dev shops |
| 4 | **The Agent Economy** — agents that hire, and the legitimacy story | **Episode 1 publishes** + 8 derived assets. Month-1 report |

**Engagement — the half nobody budgets for.** Founders comment on 10 ICP posts per week, substantively. A zero-follower page grows by borrowing audiences in other people's comment sections. Target list in `02-linkedin/engagement-playbook.md`.

**Targets:** 300–600 followers · 8–15k impressions · 2–4% engagement · baseline set

---

## Month 2 — Traction & Amplification (Weeks 5–8)

**Goal:** double down on what month 1 proved; start converting reach into contacts.

- **Rebalance the calendar around the data.** The pillar that carried month 1 goes to 35–40%; the weakest drops.
- **Podcast to fortnightly** — episodes 2 and 3. Guests from the ecosystem and the future-of-work world. Every guest amplifies to their own audience: that *is* the growth mechanism.
- **First lead magnet:** *"The Scoped Work Checklist — 12 things to agree before you hire anyone."* Deliberately vendor-neutral so it travels, and it maps exactly onto what the product does.
- **Video to 2 clips/week** from podcast footage. Captioned, 4:5, hook in the first 1.5 seconds.
- **Employee advocacy:** a repost/comment pack to the wider team each Monday.
- **Week 7 supply-side sequence** aimed squarely at freelancers, dev shops and agencies with spare capacity.

**Targets:** 800–1,500 followers · 30–60k impressions · 3–5% engagement · 3–8 qualified conversations · 50–150 emails

---

## Month 3 — Conversion & Compounding (Weeks 9–12)

**Goal:** turn an audience into pipeline, and hand over a system that runs without heroics.

- **Conversion layer:** warm-DM playbook (never cold-pitch — reference their comment or a post they engaged with), founder "open calendar" post, a clear route from post → first comment → booked call.
- **Proof content:** first user stories and walkthroughs — real numbers only, nothing invented.
- **Podcast weekly** if the pipeline supports it (episodes 4–6), and start inviting **buyer-side guests** — an agency owner or ops lead who commissions work constantly. That conversation is a sales call neither side has to call a sales call.
- **Flagship asset:** *"State of Agent Work"* — a genuinely useful data-backed report. The thing that makes the page worth following rather than scrolling past.
- **Day-90 retrospective:** honest numbers, what worked, and a 6-month plan.

**Targets:** 1,500–3,000 followers · 60–120k impressions · 4–6% engagement · 10–25 qualified conversations · 5–12 booked calls

## The podcast engine

**One 45-minute recording → 8–12 LinkedIn assets:** episode announce + guest quote card ·
4 × clips (45–90s, captioned, 4:5) · takeaway carousel · 2 × quote cards · founder text
post · the guest's own repost · audiogram · newsletter version.

**And it's a business-development channel.** The invite list *is* the target list. "Come
on the podcast" gets a far higher reply rate than a cold DM, because it's a gift rather
than an ask. Six episodes in 90 days is six senior relationships that started with us
giving something.

**Loop:** target list (10 deep) → invite → 15-min pre-call → record → edit → clip →
guest reviews their clips → publish → guest amplifies → ask for the referral.

## Graphic design — what's included

Templated, not bespoke. That's how the volume stays affordable.
6 master templates in Week 0 · 12–16 static assets/month · 1 carousel/week · podcast
cover art and episode variants · video clip framing and captions · lead-magnet layout
(M2, M3) · page and founder banners.

Out of scope at this tier: brand identity, logo design, motion graphics, web design, print.

## What this needs to work

1. **≥2 founders/execs posting from personal profiles, 2×/week, commenting 10×/week.** The single biggest determinant of whether these bands land. LinkedIn suppresses company-page reach; from zero, a page alone will not do it.
2. **48-hour content approval**, one named approver.
3. Brand assets — logo, fonts, hexes.
4. One founder hour per week (podcast + content sync).
5. Product truth — **is Tasks publicly live?** We won't imply availability we can't confirm.
6. Analytics access and a CRM field for LinkedIn-sourced leads.

---

## Reporting

**Weekly:** one screen — top 3 posts, worst post with the diagnosis, numbers vs. last
week, what changes. **Monthly:** full dashboard against the bands, learnings, next
month's plan, one ask. **Day 90:** retrospective.

**Kill criteria, agreed up front:** if at week 6 engagement is under 2% and qualified
conversations are zero, we stop and re-plan rather than keep shipping posts. Naming it
in advance is what separates a strategy from a content subscription.
