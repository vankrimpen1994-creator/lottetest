# How We Work — the production system

_This is the core of the agency pitch. The client specifics are the worked example;
**this** is what's actually being bought._

An agency isn't hiring us because we know one client's product. It's hiring us because
it needs content plans built and executed reliably, across clients, without having to
supervise us. So the thing to show is **the machine**, not the portfolio.

---

## The five-stage system

### 1 · Intake — day 1–2
Everything starts from a structured brief, not a conversation we half-remember.

**We take:** client's goals, audience, product truth, brand assets, approver, constraints.
**We produce:** a written client profile with **every factual claim sourced**, an ICP
definition, and a list of open questions.

> **What this catches.** On this client, intake surfaced a name collision with a much
> larger company — `vapi.ai`, YC W21, $50M Series B — that makes them effectively
> invisible in LinkedIn search. Free to fix, and nobody had flagged it. That's what a
> real intake is for.

### 2 · Strategy — day 3–5
**We produce:** positioning statement, message hierarchy, five content pillars with a
fixed weekly ratio, a voice-and-tone guide with an explicit banned-words list, and a KPI
framework with target bands and the assumptions they depend on written down.

The pillar ratio is the quality control that matters most. It's what stops month two
becoming four product announcements a week.

### 3 · Production — weekly, ongoing
One job per working session, each with its own documented procedure:

| Day | Job | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Engagement plan + last week's report | Target list, drafted comments, one-screen snapshot |
| Tue–Wed | Publish, respond, record | Live posts, podcast |
| Thu | **Next week's full content batch** | 7 posts drafted, sent for approval |
| Fri | Design briefs, repurposing, scheduling | Assets specced, podcast cut into 8–12 pieces |

**We draft a week ahead, always.** Same-day drafting is where quality collapses and where
an approval delay turns into a hole in the feed.

### 4 · Quality control — before anything leaves
Every asset passes a written checklist before it goes to the client. Not vibes:

- [ ] Every number sourced. **No invented metrics, quotes, case studies or results — ever.** Missing data is marked `[NEEDS DATA]`, never estimated.
- [ ] No claim that an unreleased product has shipped
- [ ] Brand name correct in the first line and on the asset watermark
- [ ] Checked against the banned-words list
- [ ] One idea per post; hook in the first two lines
- [ ] Contrast ≥ 4.5:1, legible at thumbnail size
- [ ] Nothing touching regulation, custody or legal answered by us — escalated

### 5 · Reporting — weekly and monthly
**Weekly:** one screen. Top 3 posts, the worst post with an honest diagnosis, numbers
against last week, what we change this week.
**Monthly:** full dashboard against the target bands, pillar-by-pillar performance, what
we learned, next month's plan, one explicit ask.

Both are written so **the agency can forward them to the client unedited.**

## What makes this transferable

Nothing above is vAPI-specific. Swap the client profile and the pillars, and the same
system runs for any client the agency has. That's the pitch: **not a freelancer who knows
one product, a production system you can point at anything.**

| Layer | Client-specific? |
|---|---|
| Intake questions, sourcing discipline | No — universal |
| Strategy artefacts (pillars, voice, KPIs) | Structure universal, content per client |
| Weekly production rhythm | No — universal |
| QC checklist | No — universal |
| Reporting templates | No — universal |
| Client profile, positioning, post bank | Yes |

## How we use AI — the honest version

Worth saying plainly, because the agency will wonder and because it explains the price.

**AI does:** research and source-gathering, first drafts at volume, repurposing a
transcript into a dozen assets, formatting, checklist passes.
**People do:** strategy, judgement calls, the guest relationships, client conversations,
and a review of **every single word** before it reaches the agency.

The system is documented to the point that the tooling is reproducible — the recurring
jobs (weekly content, engagement plans, podcast repurposing, design briefs, reporting)
each run from a written procedure, and every session is logged automatically.

**What the agency gets from that:** volume at a price that shouldn't be possible, with
an audit trail of how every deliverable was produced. **What it doesn't change:** a human
is accountable for everything that ships.

## Working with the agency

- **We're invisible.** The agency owns the client relationship. White-label by default — our name appears nowhere unless the agency wants it to.
- **Single point of contact** on our side. No account-manager chain.
- **48-hour turnaround** on revisions within scope. Two rounds included per asset.
- **One named approver** on the client side, with 48 hours to respond. Unreturned means approved — agreed up front so the calendar can't be blocked by silence.
- **We never contact the client's audience without per-item approval.** Drafting is always safe; publishing never happens unilaterally.
- **Bad news travels fast.** If a month is under target, it's in the first three lines of the report, not buried on page two.

## What we'd need to start on any client
1. Client goals, in the client's own words
2. Brand assets — logo, fonts, hexes
3. One named approver with a 48-hour SLA
4. Page admin access and analytics
5. Product truth — what's live, what isn't, what we can't say
6. Whether founders/execs will post from personal profiles _(this determines whether the numbers are realistic — see the KPI framework)_
